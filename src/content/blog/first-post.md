The morning came softly over the hill, putting a pale gold edge on every leaf. I set out without a map, which is the best way to discover how many lanes begin just beyond the garden gate.

The road wandered between low stone walls and gardens heavy with late summer. A baker was already at work, and the smell of warm bread followed me farther than the last chimney. No grand event marked the day. There was only a blackbird in the hedge, a gate left open for a neighbor, and the pleasant knowledge that supper waited at home.

I had thought a journey needed distance to count as an adventure. By noon, with a pocket full of apples and nowhere urgent to be, I began to suspect otherwise.
