Rain found me a mile before Bree and kept pace all the way to the first lantern. By then the road had turned dark beneath the trees, and every puddle held a broken reflection of the evening sky.

The inn was loud with boots drying by the hearth and chairs scraping over the floor. Nobody asked for a tale before offering a bowl of stew. I was given a place near the fire, directions for the morning, and a warning that the western path washed out after hard rain.

Outside, the lanterns marked the road one by one. I wrote down their number before sleep, though I doubt I will ever need it. Some details are worth keeping simply because they made the night feel less strange.
